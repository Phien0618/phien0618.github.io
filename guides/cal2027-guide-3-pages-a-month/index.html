Why 3 pages a month is easier to keep up with than 100

A planner that asks for daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly pages only works when life is calm. On a busy or scattered week, the daily pages are the first thing you skip, and then the whole book feels "behind". If you have ADHD, or you just get overwhelmed by empty pages, that feeling is often what makes you stop.

A monthly system has fewer places to fall behind. You touch it on the 1st, glance at it during the month, and close it on the last day. Each month gets three pages:

  1. Month calendar with every 2027 date already in the right weekday box, plus "This month's 3 things", "Bills & due dates" and "Don't forget".
  2. Habit & mood tracker with the real dates and weekday letters printed, so you never draw 31 columns again.
  3. 5-minute month reset: what worked, what didn't, what to carry forward and one thing to drop.

That is the whole month. Everything else is optional.

Step 1: On the 1st of the month (10 minutes)

Pick this month's 3 things

Under the calendar grid there are exactly three boxes. Write the three things that would make this month a good month, for example "Renew passport", "Walk 10 min" and "Clear the desk". Three is a limit on purpose: anything else you get done is a bonus, not a failure.

Write bills and dates once

Copy fixed bills into the 5-row "Bills & due dates" table (date, what, amount, paid). Then add up to 4 things you can't forget, such as a birthday or a renewal. Put appointments straight into their day box.

Set up the habit tracker

Choose a few habits, not ten. Because the dates are pre-printed, setup is just writing habit names on the left.

Step 2: During the month (30 seconds a day)

  • Tick habits when you remember. A blank day isn't a reason to quit; the page says it plainly: never miss twice.
  • Use the tiny version on low-energy days. The tracker has a "tiny version" box: write the smallest version of a habit (one glass of water, two minutes of tidying) so a bad day still counts.
  • Glance at the 3 things once a week. If one is clearly not happening, that's information for the reset, not a reason for guilt.
For the hard days: the full calendar adds two undated bonus pages. Brain dump (page 42): write every thought on its own line, mark it Today, Later or Drop, then pick the next 3 actions and one first step small enough to start in 2 minutes. Minimum version (page 43): on a good day, write the smallest version of food, body, home, work, money, people and rest, so a hard day already has a plan. Print them as often as you need.

Step 3: On the last day (5 minutes)

Open the month reset and answer four short prompts: what worked, what didn't, what to carry forward (5 rows, no guilt) and one thing to drop. Then turn the page: the reset ends with "Next: February", so you go straight into Step 1 for the new month.

Check a 2027 calendar before you trust it

Free calendar generators are fine, but check two dates before you print twelve months: January 1, 2027 is a Friday, and February 2027 has 28 days. Also note that no calendar can print everyone's holidays correctly. This one prints none, so add the ones your country and family keep in the "Don't forget" list.
