Freelance late payment emails: a reminder sequence that gets invoices paid

Most templates for overdue invoices give you one polite reminder and one "final notice". Real late payments usually need a sequence. Each message gets a bit firmer, and each one makes it easier for the client to pay than to ignore you. Below you'll find the full sequence from three days before the due date to 30 days late, replies to the excuses you'll hear, and a calculator for late fees.

The sequence at a glance

Two rules apply to every email: always attach the invoice or link to it again (never make them search for it), and always end with one clear question, usually "When can I expect payment?". An email without a question is easy to leave unanswered.

Email 1: three days before the due date

This is the email most freelancers skip, and it's the one that heads off the most late payments. It surfaces problems while there's still time: the invoice went to the wrong person, accounts payable needs a PO number, or the client's payment run is next week.

Subject: Invoice [#] for [project] — due [date] Hi [Name], Quick heads-up that invoice [#] for [amount] is due on [date]. I've attached it again here, and you can pay via [link / bank details]. If it needs to go to someone else or needs a PO number, just let me know and I'll resend it today. Thanks, [Your name]

Email 2: on the due date

Subject: Invoice [#] due today Hi [Name], Just a reminder that invoice [#] for [amount] is due today. Payment link: [link]. The invoice is attached too. Thanks again for the work on [project]. It was a good one. [Your name]

Email 3: three days late

Assume it's an oversight, because it usually is. But ask for a date, not just "a look".

Subject: Invoice [#] — now 3 days overdue Hi [Name], I haven't seen payment for invoice [#] ([amount], due [date]) yet. Could you check where it's at and let me know when I can expect it? Invoice attached, payment link here: [link]. Thanks, [Your name]

Email 4: ten days late

Get firmer, and go one level wider: copy the accounts or finance contact if you have one. The goal is a specific date in writing.

Subject: Overdue: invoice [#] ([amount]) — 10 days past due Hi [Name], Invoice [#] for [amount] was due on [date] and is now 10 days overdue. I've followed up on [date] but haven't had a reply. Could you confirm the date payment will be sent? If something is blocking it (a missing detail on the invoice, an approval step), tell me what you need and I'll sort it out the same day. [cc: accounts contact] [Your name]

Email 5: 21 days late (pause work, mention the fee)

If you're still working for this client, this is the point where you stop starting new work. Say it plainly, without drama. Only mention a late fee if your contract or invoice terms included one.

Subject: Invoice [#] — 21 days overdue, work paused Hi [Name], Invoice [#] ([amount], due [date]) is now three weeks overdue, and I haven't received a payment date. Per our agreement, I'm pausing work on [current task] until the invoice is settled, and a late fee of [amount / rate] applies from [date]. As soon as payment arrives I'll pick things up right away. You can pay here: [link]. If there's a problem I don't know about, please call me at [number] and we can work it out. [Your name]

Email 6: final notice (30+ days)

Give a specific deadline and a specific next step. Only name a step you're actually willing to take, such as a formal demand letter, a small claims filing or a collections agency. Empty threats teach clients to ignore your deadlines.

Subject: Final notice: invoice [#] — payment due by [date] Hi [Name], This is a final notice for invoice [#] for [amount], originally due [date] and now [X] days overdue. Including the late fee in our agreement, the balance is [total]. If payment isn't received by [date, 7 days out], I'll [send a formal demand letter / file a small claims case / pass the debt to a collections agency]. I'd much rather not, and I'm open to a short payment plan if that helps. Just reply with what you can do. Payment link: [link] [Your name]

Replies to the usual excuses

"It's with accounts / in the next payment run."

Thanks, that helps. When is the next payment run, and is there a contact in accounts I can check with directly so I don't need to bother you about it?

"We're waiting for our client to pay us."

I understand, and that's frustrating. Our agreement isn't tied to that payment, though. Could we agree on a date this month? If cash flow is tight, I can do two instalments: [half] by [date] and the rest by [date].

"We weren't happy with part of the work."

I'm sorry to hear that, and I'm glad you told me. Can you tell me specifically what isn't right? If it's within the agreed scope, I'll fix it. In the meantime, could you pay for the parts you're happy with ([items], [amount]) so the rest doesn't hold everything up?

Silence after three or more emails

Change channel. Call, or send a short message through whatever tool you used during the project. "Hi [Name], just checking my invoice emails are reaching you, I haven't heard back on [#]." Sometimes the email really has gone to spam.

Late fee calculator

Use this to work out a late fee that's in your agreement: a flat fee, a monthly interest rate charged per day, or both. It only does the arithmetic. Whether you can charge it depends on your contract and local law (see the note below).

Formula: interest = amount × (monthly rate ÷ 100) × (days ÷ 30), simple interest, not compounded. Example: 2,400 × 0.015 × 45/30 = 54.00, so the new balance is 2,454.00.

A note on late fees

A late fee is much easier to collect if it was written in your contract or on the invoice before the work started. Adding one afterwards usually gets you an argument, not a payment. Rules differ by country and state. For example, the UK lets businesses claim statutory interest on late commercial payments, and some places cap interest rates. Check your local rules (or ask an accountant) before relying on a number. This guide isn't legal advice.

Prevent the next one

  • Take a deposit. 30–50% up front on new clients filters out most of the ones who won't pay.
  • Bill at milestones on anything longer than a month, so you're never owed more than one stage of work.
  • Put the terms on the quote: due date (e.g. 14 days), the late fee, and "work pauses when an invoice is 14+ days overdue". Then email 5 is a reminder, not a new rule.
  • Ask who pays invoices at kickoff, and send invoices to that person as well as your day-to-day contact.
  • Hand over final files after final payment on fixed-price projects. Show previews until then.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting weeks before the first reminder. The longer you wait, the more it signals that you don't care.
  • Apologising for asking. "Sorry to bother you" makes an unpaid invoice sound like an imposition. You did the work, so be plain about it.
  • Carrying on working while unpaid. Every extra week of work increases what you could lose.
  • Getting angry in writing. Stay factual. If it ever goes to small claims, your emails are the evidence.
