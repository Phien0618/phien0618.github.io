How do you run a murder mystery dinner party for 6 with a printable game?

Give each course one round: starter = Round 1, main = Round 2, dessert = Round 3. And here's the twist that makes it work for a small table: the host doesn't know who did it either, because the answer waits in a sealed envelope until dessert is cleared.

The short answer

1. Each course opens one round: the host reads a short script, reveals three evidence cards, and every guest opens the next section of their booklet.

2. The main course is the longest, so Round 2 gets the most time (35 minutes, against 25 for the starter and dessert).

3. After dessert everyone writes an accusation, then any guest opens the sealed answer and reads it aloud, so the host gets to guess as well.

2. The story at the table (no spoilers)

Autumn, the 1930s. Bartholomew "Barty" Thistle, a retired jam magnate, has invited a few old friends to the Harvest Supper at Thistledown House. Before the soup was served, Barty took a glass of elderflower cordial in the study, sat down for a rest and never woke up. Someone at the table knows why.

The suspects are the family's heir, the family doctor, the steward who has run the house for twenty-five years, the family solicitor, a food critic, the head gardener who makes the cordial every June, a newspaper reporter and a visiting chef. Every character has a motive, an opportunity and a red herring. Nothing is gruesome: no injuries are described, there is no romance and nobody is asked to drink.

The starter cards set the scene with three physical things (a cordial glass, a garden ledger and a solicitor's envelope). The main course and dessert bring six more cards and a new section of every booklet, so stories that sounded fine over soup can start to wobble by the main. We won't say more than that here.

How much does an optional role get at 8 players? The same as a core role: a 4-page booklet with a public cover, a secret page, and for each course things to share, two questions for named suspects and a small task. The difference is what their information does. The solution can be reached from the four core booklets and the nine evidence cards, so optional players add suspects, alibis and red herrings rather than carrying a clue nobody else has. That is why the game still works if an optional guest cancels, and why a quieter guest can take an optional role without stalling the case.

4. For experienced hosts

  • Seat for friction. Each Round asks guests questions for named suspects. Seat those pairs across the table from each other, not side by side, so questions happen in front of everyone.
  • Use the course change as a beat. Clear plates, dim the lights for ten seconds, then read the next script before serving. The pause makes the new round feel like a scene change.
  • Run the evidence like a reveal. Keep each course's three cards in their own envelope and turn them over one at a time, reading only the title first.
  • Let the cordial be a prop. A jug of elderflower cordial (or any pale soft drink) on the sideboard costs little and gives the table something to point at.
  • Short on time? The Host Guide includes a 75-minute version: 5 / 5 for welcome and introductions, 15 / 20 / 15 for the three rounds, 5 for ballots and 10 for the reveal.

6. Free host checklist

One week before

  • Pick the date: full evening (about 2 hours 20 minutes) or the 75-minute version.
  • Confirm your guest count and find your row in the casting table.
  • Send invitations with each guest's character and a costume idea.
  • Plan three simple courses. Shop-bought food is fine; the mystery is the entertainment.

Two or three days before

  • Give each guest only their own booklet.
  • Print and cut cards, name tags and ballots.
  • Put the evidence cards into three envelopes: Starter, Main, Dessert.
  • Seal the solution, unread, in a fourth envelope marked "Open after the vote."

On the day

  • Set place cards and keep a spot in the middle of the table for evidence.
  • Put out pens and one detective-notes sheet per guest.
  • Keep the scripts, the three envelopes and the ballots by your seat.

7. Food that works with a mystery

Choose dishes that can be plated or served from one pot, so you are not stuck in the kitchen when a round starts: a soup for the starter, a roast or tray bake for the main, and a crumble or tart that sits in a warm oven until dessert.

Common questions

I planned for 6, but only 4 can come. Does it still work?
Yes. The four core booklets and nine evidence cards hold the complete case; use booklets 10–13 only.

Is it OK for teens or grandparents?
Yes, it is a cozy game: nothing gruesome, no romance and no drinking tasks.

How much reading is there?
Each round's section of a booklet is under 180 words, so guests read in about a minute and get back to talking.
