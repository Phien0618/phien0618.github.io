Which overdue invoice email template should I send?

Count the days first. A client who is 3 days late needs a different email from one who is 4 weeks late, and sending the wrong one either sounds rude or gets ignored. Below is a 4-step ladder by days overdue, with the full wording of each email to copy.

The ladder: days overdue → which email

Invoice statusSend thisTone
Due in 3 days or less1Heads-upBefore it's late
1–7 days late2Friendly nudgeAssume it slipped
8–21 days late3Firm reminderDays late + a pay-by date
22+ days late4Final noticeNext step + deadline

How to count: days overdue = today's date minus the due date. An invoice due on October 5 is 4 days overdue on October 9, so it gets a friendly nudge. If you have more than one late invoice, start with the most overdue one.

The 4 overdue invoice email templates

This is the wording of the four reminder templates in the Freelance Client & Invoice Tracker. In the workbook the placeholders are filled in automatically; here they are shown in [brackets] for you to replace. Adapt the wording to your contract and the rules where you and your client are.

1Heads-up (due in 3 days or less)

Subject: Invoice [invoice #] is due on [due date] Hi [first name], a quick heads-up that invoice [invoice #] for [amount] is due on [due date]. Everything you need to pay is on the invoice. Thanks, [your name]

2Friendly nudge (1–7 days late)

Subject: Quick reminder: invoice [invoice #] Hi [first name], invoice [invoice #] for [amount] was due on [due date], [days] days ago. Could you let me know when it's scheduled? If it's already on its way, thank you, and please ignore this. Best, [your name]

3Firm reminder (8–21 days late)

Subject: Invoice [invoice #] is now [days] days overdue Hi [first name], invoice [invoice #] for [amount] was due on [due date] and is now [days] days overdue. Please pay the balance of [amount] by [pay-by date], or reply with the date it will be paid. Thank you, [your name]

4Final notice (22+ days late)

Subject: Final notice: invoice [invoice #] Hi [first name], invoice [invoice #] is now [days] days overdue and I haven't had a reply to my earlier reminders. If the balance of [amount] isn't paid by [pay-by date], I'll pause any further work. If there's a problem with the invoice, please reply today so we can sort it out. Regards, [your name]

Late fee (optional): only if your contract or terms already include one, extend the final notice sentence to "…I'll pause any further work and add [your late-fee wording] as set out in my terms." Adding a fee that was never agreed usually starts an argument, and the rules on late fees differ by country and state.

Why the wording changes at each step

  • Heads-up: no pressure at all. It catches the boring problems (invoice sent to the wrong person, a missing PO number) before the due date passes.
  • Friendly nudge: most invoices a few days late were simply forgotten. The email asks a question ("when is it scheduled?") instead of making a demand, and gives the client an easy way out if the payment is already on its way.
  • Firm reminder: after a week, a vague "any update?" is easy to ignore. This step names the exact number of days and a pay-by date. A date about five days out is enough time for a normal payment run.
  • Final notice: it states one consequence you can actually carry out, pausing further work, plus a deadline. It still leaves a door open ("if there's a problem with the invoice…"), because a disputed invoice needs a conversation, not another reminder.

Two habits make every step work better: send each email as a reply in the same thread so the history is visible, and attach or link the invoice again so nobody has to search for it.
