The story

It is Christmas Eve at Snowbury Lodge, and the Frost & Fable Toy Company is having its annual party. Founder Nicholas "Nick" Frost raises his favourite red mug for the 9 o'clock toast. Forty minutes later he collapses beside the tree. The snow has closed the only road, nobody can leave, and somebody in the room did it.

Your guests arrive in costume as the head toymaker, the finance director, the struggling-novelist heir, the caterer, an uninvited rival and more. Every character has a motive, a secret, public relationships and goals for the night. Each booklet also tells its player how to play the part (Casey bustles about with a ladle and takes any slight against the eggnog personally), gives three lines to try and says what to answer when someone points the finger. Each round, you reveal new evidence cards and every player opens the next section of their booklet. It gives them something to reveal or gossip about, questions to put to named suspects, and a mini task, such as taking a "newsletter photo" of three suspects or handing out a naughty-or-nice verdict as Santa. The six optional characters are eyewitnesses. Each one holds a detail that backs up the evidence, so a bigger party solves the case faster.

You play Inspector Noel and don't take any of the 12 roles. The 15-page Host Guide takes you from zero. It has a tick-box checklist, a one-hour prep plan, a cast table for 6 to 16 guests and a removable-characters table. It also has a full run sheet, a 90-minute Express run sheet and four read-aloud scripts. Each script comes with interrogation prompts and optional follow-up questions for quiet moments. Hosting tips, a themed menu and decor ideas round it out.

The first round's evidence points at the wrong people on purpose. The answer only comes together once the key log, the Secret Santa list, the receipts and the last round of evidence are on the table. The sealed solution lays out the 6-step clue chain with the card numbers for each step, explains why each of the other suspects is innocent, and gives you a word-for-word reveal script and a ballot answer key.

Costumes come in two levels for every role: an easy one from your wardrobe, and a "go all out" country-lodge look (velvet, tweed, white fur, a Santa suit) for the photos. Every page reads clearly in black and white, and every PDF comes in true US Letter and A4 sizes.

FAQ

How many people can play?
6–16. Up to 12 guests each get their own role; with 13–16, guests share roles in pairs or play Deputy Detectives (they get their own brief). The host plays Inspector Noel on top of that.

How long does it take?
About 2.5 hours with a dinner break, or about 90 minutes with the Express version.

How much printing and cutting?
For 6 players the essentials are 28 pages and 12 evidence cards. Invitations can be sent by phone, and name tags, decor and awards are optional.

Is a 4-page booklet enough for a whole evening?
Each booklet is built for mingling, not reading aloud. Page 1 is the character and costume. Page 2 is the secret, how to play the part, three lines to try and what to say if accused. Pages 3 and 4 hold three rounds, each with things to reveal, two questions for named suspects and a mini task, plus a conversation tracker and notes. Most of the evening is spent talking, and the booklet keeps every player supplied with something to say.
