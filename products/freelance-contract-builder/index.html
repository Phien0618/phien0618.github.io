3. Word for word from guide Chapter 5, "When the client pushes back" (3 of the 6 objections):

Client: "Can we skip the deposit? We always pay on completion."
Your reply: I ask every client for a deposit because it reserves your start date and my time. I can do 30% instead of 50% if that helps your cash flow, with the balance on the same date.
Why it works: it explains the deposit as a booking, not a lack of trust, and offers a smaller number instead of zero.

Client: "Our standard payment terms are Net 60."
Your reply: I understand larger teams work that way. As a small business I invoice Net 14. If Net 60 is fixed on your side, I can accept Net 30 with a 50% deposit, or Net 60 with the price adjusted to cover the wait.
Why it works: you give two concrete options, and both protect your cash flow.

Client: "Please remove the kill fee. We won't cancel."
Your reply: Then the clause will never cost you anything. It is there because I turn down other work to hold your dates. I can lower it from 25% to 15% if that makes it easier to sign.
Why it works: the client's own confidence becomes the reason the clause is harmless.

4. Word for word from guide Chapter 6, "12 red-flag clauses" (what to look for when the client sends their own contract; 4 of the 12):

Red flag (their wording)Ask for this instead
"Payment is due 60 days after the client approves the invoice."A fixed number of days after delivery (Net 14 or 30), not after an approval that may never come.
"The client may terminate this agreement at any time without further payment."Payment for work done plus a kill fee if they cancel for convenience.
"All rights in the work transfer to the client upon creation."Ownership transfers when the full fee is paid; a license to use the work until then.

Why a contract builder

Most freelance contract templates are a static document: you retype names, prices and dates every time, and the contract, the scope and the change order slowly stop agreeing with each other. This builder works the other way round.

Open contract-builder.html in your browser, pick a preset and fill in four short cards: you, your client, the project and the fees. The builder writes three documents from those details:

  • Freelance Services Agreement with 20 numbered clauses, a key-facts strip (total, deposit, balance due, delivery) and typed-name signature lines.
  • Statement of Work with project overview, deliverables, a "Not included" list, milestones and dates, acceptance window, payment schedule and revision rounds (plus hours cap, minimum term and notice period for retainers).
  • Change Order with an automatic number, links to the agreement and SOW, and a before / change / after table for price, balance, delivery date and balance due date.

The numbers are calculated, not retyped. In the example preset, a $3,200 fixed fee with a 50% deposit, Net 14 and a 25% kill fee gives a $1,600 deposit, a $1,600 balance due Dec 28, 2026 and an $800 kill fee, and those figures appear identically in the Agreement and the SOW. A change order for +$450 and +5 days moves the total to $3,650 and the balance due date to Jan 2, 2027.

Each of 12 clauses has three wordings. Flexible gives the client more room, Standard is the fair default, Firm spells out consequences for late payment or late materials. Turn on "Show plain-English notes" to see what each clause protects you from.

You can add your logo and brand color, switch between US Letter and A4, print all three documents (each starts on a new page) or just one, copy any document as plain text, save up to 20 contracts in your browser and back them up with Export / Import JSON. It works offline in any modern browser; print footer page numbers appear in Chrome/Edge. Nothing is uploaded.
