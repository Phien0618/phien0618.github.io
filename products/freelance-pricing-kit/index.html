Why it's worth $12 when free rate calculators exist

Free rate calculators are everywhere. What they don't tell you is what to say when the client answers "that's a bit much", or what happens to your income in the months after you raise your rate. This kit covers the whole path: your numbers, your rate, a quote you can send, the words for when they push back, and the plan for charging more.

Start with your floor rate. Type your income goal, business costs, tax rate and an honest share of billable hours into the workbook (or the offline browser calculator). You get the lowest hourly and day rate that still pays you. In the worked example, Maya, a brand designer, needs 94,600 a year. Spread over her 1,056 real billable hours, that makes her floor 90 an hour. Divide the same 94,600 by the usual 2,080 hours (52 weeks × 40) and you'd get about 45, half of what she needs.

Turn it into three packages. List the project's tasks and tag each one Essential, Standard or Premium. The 3-Tier Quote tab adds contingency, rounds up, and gives you prices, deposits and balances. Maya's brand refresh comes out at 1,900 / 2,700 / 3,250, with 50% deposits of 950 / 1,350 / 1,625. Not a designer? Chapter 2 shows the same method for a Shopify store build, a website copy project and a marketing audit.

Send a quote that looks agency-grade. Paste the prices into the quote template, click any yellow field to edit it, and save it as a PDF. Deposits are calculated from each price and your deposit %. It has a validity date, a "Recommended" package, a "Not included" list and a signature block, so the client can say yes in writing.

Keep your price when they push back. Chapter 4 covers eight things clients say about price. Each one has the weak reply to avoid and a word-for-word reply that changes the scope instead of your rate. Chapter 3 adds the scope-of-work section, a change order, a change log and six scope-creep replies.

Raise your rates, and see the months after. Chapter 5 starts with the break-even (how much work you could lose and still earn the same), then stress-tests it month by month: lose more clients than break-even, refill the hours slowly, and see the lowest point of your bank balance. Three emails cover existing clients, new clients and retainer clients, with replies to six likely responses, including "can you phase it in?" and no reply at all.

Ongoing work and per-word rates. Chapter 6 and calculator Step 4 show what a per-word rate really pays per hour (in the example, 0.12 a word works out to 30 an hour), the minimum per-word rate at your floor, and three monthly retainer tiers with the terms to write down.

Check it yourself before you trust it. Every number in the guide matches the workbook defaults and the calculator. Amounts are currency-neutral: use dollars, pounds, euros or anything else.
