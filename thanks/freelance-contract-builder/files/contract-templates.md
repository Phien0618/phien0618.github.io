# Freelance Contract & SOW Templates (Markdown)

Three blank templates for Google Docs, Word or Notion: **Freelance Services Agreement**, **Statement of Work** and **Change Order**. They contain the same clause text as `contract-builder.html`, with every wording version (Flexible, Standard, Firm) and every billing variant (fixed, hourly, monthly retainer).

How to use:
1. Copy a template into your document editor.
2. For each clause, keep ONE wording and delete the others. Keep the billing variant that matches how you charge.
3. Replace every `[Placeholder]` with your details (search for `[` to find any you missed).
4. Delete the "What this protects you from" notes before sending, or keep them for your own copy.

**Not legal advice. Not written by a lawyer. Have a local lawyer review before signing.** All examples are fictional.

# Template 1: Freelance Services Agreement

**[Freelancer Business]** · [Freelancer Name] · [Freelancer Email]
SERVICES AGREEMENT · [Agreement Ref] · Between [Freelancer Business] and [Client Company]

## Freelance Services Agreement
[Project Name] · dated [Agreement Date] · see Statement of Work of the same date

| Total fee | Deposit [Deposit %] | Balance due | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Total Fee] | [Deposit Amount] | [Balance Amount] · [Balance Due Date] | [Delivery Date] |

### 1. Parties
This agreement is between [Freelancer Business] (“I”, “me”), [Freelancer Email], and [Client Company], represented by [Client Contact], [Client Title] (“you”). It is dated [Agreement Date] and has the reference [Agreement Ref].

> What this protects you from: Confusion about who owes you the money. Naming the client's business, not just the person you talk to, makes it clear who is bound.

### 2. Services & Statement of Work
I will provide the [Service Description] listed in the Statement of Work (SOW) dated [Agreement Date] for [Project Name]. The SOW is part of this agreement. Anything not listed there, including the items under “Not included”, is outside this agreement.

> What this protects you from: Arguments about what was “obviously included”. The SOW is the written list both of you can point to.

### 3. Fees & Deposit
**Fixed fee**
- Flexible: The fixed fee is [Total Fee]. A deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %]) is due when you sign and is credited to the total. If you cancel before work starts, I refund the deposit minus the cost of any work already done.
- Standard: The fixed fee is [Total Fee]. A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %]) is due when you sign. Work starts once the deposit is received.
- Firm: The fixed fee is [Total Fee]. A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %]) is due when you sign. Your start date of [Start Date] is reserved only once the deposit has cleared; if it arrives late, the start date and the delivery date move by the same number of days.

**Hourly**
- Flexible: My rate is [Hourly Rate] per hour. The estimate is [Estimated Hours] hours ([Total Fee]). A deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the estimate) is due when you sign and is credited to the final invoice. If you cancel before work starts, I refund the deposit minus the cost of any work already done.
- Standard: My rate is [Hourly Rate] per hour. The estimate is [Estimated Hours] hours ([Total Fee]). A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the estimate) is due when you sign and is credited to the final invoice. I will ask you in writing before the work goes more than 10% over the estimate.
- Firm: My rate is [Hourly Rate] per hour, tracked in 15-minute blocks. The estimate is [Estimated Hours] hours ([Total Fee]). A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the estimate) is due when you sign and is credited to the final invoice. Work starts once the deposit has cleared. Work beyond the estimate continues only after you approve a new estimate in writing.

**Monthly retainer**
- Flexible: The fee is [Monthly Fee] per month for up to [Monthly Hours] hours of work. Extra hours are billed at [Overage Rate] per hour if you approve them. A deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the first month) is due when you sign and is credited to the first monthly invoice. Unused hours roll over to the next month once, then expire.
- Standard: The fee is [Monthly Fee] per month for up to [Monthly Hours] hours of work. Extra hours are billed at [Overage Rate] per hour, only after you approve them in writing (email is fine). A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the first month) is due when you sign and is credited to the first monthly invoice. Unused hours do not roll over.
- Firm: The fee is [Monthly Fee] per month for up to [Monthly Hours] hours of work. Extra hours are billed at [Overage Rate] per hour after your written approval. The minimum term is [Minimum Months] months ([Minimum Total]). A non-refundable deposit of [Deposit Amount] ([Deposit %] of the first month) is due when you sign and is credited to the first monthly invoice. Unused hours do not roll over and are not refunded.

> What this protects you from: Doing weeks of work for a client who disappears. The deposit means you are never fully unpaid, and it shows the client is committed.

### 4. Payment Terms & Late Fee
**Fixed fee or hourly** (for hourly, write the balance as "[Balance Amount] (estimated)")
- Flexible: The balance of [Balance Amount] is due [Net Days] days after delivery, on [Balance Due Date]. If a payment is more than 14 days late, a late fee of [Late Fee %] per month applies to the unpaid amount.
- Standard: The balance of [Balance Amount] is due [Net Days] days after delivery, on [Balance Due Date]. Late invoices carry a fee of [Late Fee %] per month on the unpaid amount.
- Firm: The balance of [Balance Amount] is due [Net Days] days after delivery, on [Balance Due Date]. Late invoices carry a fee of [Late Fee %] per month on the unpaid amount, from the day after the due date. If a payment is more than 14 days late, I may pause all work until the account is paid, and deadlines move by the length of the pause.

**Monthly retainer**
- Flexible: I invoice [Monthly Fee] at the start of each month for that month's work. Invoices are due within [Net Days] days. If a payment is more than 14 days late, a late fee of [Late Fee %] per month applies to the unpaid amount.
- Standard: I invoice [Monthly Fee] on the first working day of each month for that month's work, due within [Net Days] days. Approved extra hours are added to the next monthly invoice. Late invoices carry a fee of [Late Fee %] per month on the unpaid amount.
- Firm: I invoice [Monthly Fee] on the first working day of each month for that month's work, due within [Net Days] days. Approved extra hours are added to the next monthly invoice. Late invoices carry a fee of [Late Fee %] per month on the unpaid amount. If an invoice is more than 14 days late, I may pause work until the account is paid; paused days still count toward the minimum term.

> What this protects you from: “We'll pay when we can.” A fixed date plus a late fee turns a vague promise into a deadline. Late-fee limits differ by place, so check yours.

### 5. Revisions
- Flexible: The fee includes [Revision Rounds] rounds of revisions. Small fixes after that, such as typos or spacing, are free; larger extra rounds are [Extra Round Price] each.
- Standard: The fee includes [Revision Rounds] rounds of revisions on the chosen direction. Each extra round is [Extra Round Price], agreed in writing before I start it.
- Firm: The fee includes [Revision Rounds] rounds of revisions on the chosen direction. A round is one set of combined feedback sent in a single message. Each extra round is [Extra Round Price], paid before I start it. Changing direction after approval is a change request, not a revision.

> What this protects you from: Endless “one more tweak” rounds. A set number of rounds, and a price for extra ones, ends the loop politely.

### 6. Change Requests
- Flexible: If you want to add or change work, I will tell you the price and time impact by email before I start. Your written OK by email is enough.
- Standard: New work or changes to the SOW are made with a numbered Change Order that states the price and time impact. I start changed work after we both sign it.
- Firm: New work or changes to the SOW are made only with a numbered Change Order that states the price and time impact. I start changed work after we both sign it and any extra deposit stated in it is paid. Until then, the original scope, price and dates apply.

> What this protects you from: “Can you just add…” scope creep. Every extra has a price and a date before you do it.

### 7. Client Responsibilities & Delays
**Fixed fee or hourly**
- Flexible: You will send the materials and feedback I ask for within a reasonable time, normally 5 business days. If materials you owe me arrive late, the delivery date moves by the same number of days. The final payment stays due on the original date ([Balance Due Date]).
- Standard: You will send the materials, access and feedback I ask for within 3 business days of my request. If materials you owe me arrive late, the delivery date moves by the same number of days. The final payment stays due on the original date ([Balance Due Date]).
- Firm: You will send the materials, access and feedback I ask for within 2 business days of my request. If materials you owe me arrive late, the delivery date moves by the same number of days, plus up to 5 business days for me to fit the work back into my schedule. The final payment stays due on the original date ([Balance Due Date]).

**Monthly retainer**
- Flexible: You will send the materials and feedback I ask for within a reasonable time, normally 5 business days. If materials you owe me arrive late, the work that depends on them moves by the same number of days. Monthly invoices stay due on their normal dates.
- Standard: You will send the materials, access and feedback I ask for within 3 business days of my request. If materials you owe me arrive late, the work that depends on them moves by the same number of days. Monthly invoices stay due on their normal dates.
- Firm: You will send the materials, access and feedback I ask for within 2 business days of my request. If materials you owe me arrive late, the work that depends on them moves by the same number of days, plus up to 5 business days for me to fit it back into my schedule. Monthly invoices stay due on their normal dates.

> What this protects you from: Waiting weeks for content and then waiting weeks to be paid. Your deadline moves when theirs slips, but your payday does not.

### 8. Acceptance
- Flexible: After delivery, you have [Acceptance Days] days to review the work and tell me about anything that doesn't match the SOW. I will fix those items at no charge.
- Standard: After delivery, you have [Acceptance Days] days to tell me in writing about anything that doesn't match the SOW. I fix those items at no charge. If I hear nothing in that time, the work is accepted.
- Firm: After delivery, you have [Acceptance Days] days to list in writing anything that doesn't match the SOW. I fix listed items at no charge; anything else is a revision or a change request. If I hear nothing in that time, or you use the work publicly, the work is accepted.

> What this protects you from: A project that is never “finished”. A review window with a clear end turns “we're still looking at it” into a yes or a list.

### 9. Intellectual Property
- Flexible: When you have paid the full fee, you own the final delivered work. Until then, you may use the delivered work for this project under a license from me.
- Standard: Ownership of the final delivered work transfers to you once the full fee is paid. Until then, you have a license to use it for this project. Drafts, unused concepts and my own tools, templates and know-how stay mine.
- Firm: Ownership of the final delivered work transfers to you once the full fee and any signed Change Orders are paid. Until then, you have a license to use it for this project, which ends if a payment is more than 30 days late. Drafts, unused concepts and my own tools, templates and know-how stay mine.

> What this protects you from: Handing over everything and then chasing the balance. Ownership follows payment, so the client has a reason to pay on time.

### 10. Portfolio Rights
- Flexible: I may show the finished work in my portfolio after it is public. If you would rather I didn't, tell me in writing and I won't.
- Standard: I may show the finished work and a short description of the project in my portfolio and on social media after it is publicly launched, unless it contains your confidential information.
- Firm: I may show the finished work, drafts and a short case study in my portfolio, on social media and in award entries, starting 30 days after delivery. You may ask me to remove confidential details.

> What this protects you from: Doing your best work and never being allowed to show it. Your next client wants to see this one.

### 11. Confidentiality
- Flexible: I will keep your non-public business information private and use it only for this project.
- Standard: Each of us will keep the other's non-public business information private, use it only for this project, and return or delete it when asked. This does not cover information that is already public.
- Firm: Each of us will keep the other's non-public business information private during this agreement and for 2 years after it ends, use it only for this project, and return or delete it when asked. This does not cover information that is already public or that the law requires us to share.

> What this protects you from: A client who worries you will leak their plans, and a client who shares your prices or methods. Making it mutual protects you too.

### 12. Independent Contractor Status
I am an independent business, not your employee. I choose how, when and where I do the work, use my own equipment, and pay my own taxes and insurance. Nothing in this agreement creates an employment relationship or a partnership.

> What this protects you from: Being treated like staff without staff benefits: fixed hours, daily check-ins, office rules. It also makes clear who handles tax. How this is judged depends on local law.

### 13. Cancellation & Kill Fee
**Fixed fee or hourly**
- Flexible: If you cancel the project after work has started, you pay for the work done so far, up to [Kill Fee %] of the fee ([Kill Fee Amount]). I refund any part of the deposit above that amount.
- Standard: If you cancel after this agreement is signed, you owe a kill fee of [Kill Fee %] of the fee ([Kill Fee Amount]), or the value of the work completed so far if that is higher. The deposit counts toward this amount and is not refunded.
- Firm: If you cancel after this agreement is signed, you owe a kill fee of [Kill Fee %] of the fee ([Kill Fee Amount]) plus the value of all work completed so far. The deposit counts toward this amount and is not refunded. Cancellation must be in writing.

**Monthly retainer** ([Kill Fee Amount] = [Monthly Fee] × [Kill Fee %])
- Flexible: If you cancel before the start date, you owe up to [Kill Fee %] of one month's fee ([Kill Fee Amount]) for time already booked, and I refund any part of the deposit above that amount. After the start date, the Termination clause applies.
- Standard: If you cancel before the start date, you owe a kill fee of [Kill Fee %] of one month's fee ([Kill Fee Amount]). The deposit counts toward it and is not refunded. After the start date, the Termination clause applies.
- Firm: If you cancel before the start date, you owe a kill fee of [Kill Fee %] of one month's fee ([Kill Fee Amount]). The deposit counts toward it and is not refunded. After the start date, the minimum term and the Termination clause apply.

> What this protects you from: A client who cancels halfway after you turned down other work. The kill fee pays for the time you reserved.

### 14. Limitation of Liability
- Flexible: My total liability under this agreement is limited to twice the fees you paid me under it. Neither of us is liable for indirect losses, such as lost profits.
- Standard: My total liability under this agreement is limited to the fees you paid me under it. Neither of us is liable for indirect or consequential losses, such as lost profits or lost data.
- Firm: My total liability under this agreement is limited to the fees you paid me in the 3 months before the claim. Neither of us is liable for indirect or consequential losses, such as lost profits, lost data or lost business. You are responsible for checking that the final work suits your legal needs, such as trademark clearance or industry rules.

> What this protects you from: One bad day costing more than the whole project. A cap ties your worst case to what you were paid. Some losses cannot be capped by law, so check locally.

### 15–16. Profession clauses (pick the two for your field)

**Brand/Graphic Design**
- *Final Files & Source Files:* Final files are delivered on the delivery date in the formats listed in the SOW. Editable source files, such as layered working files, are included only if the SOW lists them. Ownership of delivered files transfers under the Intellectual Property clause.
  > What this protects you from: “Send me all your working files” after the project. Source files are valuable; list them in the SOW if they are part of the price.
- *Fonts, Stock Images & Printing:* Licensed fonts, stock images and printing are paid by you, at cost, after you approve them. Their licenses are in your name. I check print proofs on screen; the final printed result is the printer's responsibility.
  > What this protects you from: Surprise costs coming out of your fee, and blame for a printer's color mistake.

**Web/Shopify Development**
- *Hosting, Domains & Third-Party Tools:* You own and pay for your hosting, domain, store plan, apps and plugins directly with the providers, in your name. I am not responsible for outages, price changes or updates made by those providers.
  > What this protects you from: Being blamed, or billed, for a platform outage, an app price rise or a plugin update you did not make.
- *Support After Launch:* The fee includes 14 days of bug fixes after launch for problems in my own work. After that, support, updates and new features are quoted and billed separately.
  > What this protects you from: Being on call for free forever. A short bug-fix window is fair; ongoing care is a separate paid service.

**Copywriting/Content Writing**
- *Byline & Credit:* You may publish the work with or without my name. If you change the text substantially, you will not credit me as its sole author without asking me first. I may list the project in my portfolio under the Portfolio Rights clause.
  > What this protects you from: Your name on text someone else rewrote, or your best work published with no way to prove it is yours.
- *AI Tools:* I write the delivered text myself. I may use AI tools for research or proofreading, but not to generate the delivered text, unless we agree otherwise in writing.
  > What this protects you from: A client worrying they paid for machine-written text. Saying plainly how you use AI tools builds trust before it becomes a question.

**Marketing (retainer)**
- *Ad Spend & Paid Tools:* Advertising budgets, boosted posts and paid tools are not part of my fee. You pay them directly to the platforms, and you approve every budget in writing before it is spent.
  > What this protects you from: Ad budgets eating your fee, or a surprise bill you are expected to cover.
- *Results & Platform Rules:* I will follow good practice, but I cannot promise specific results such as followers, leads or sales. Platforms can change their rules or restrict accounts at any time, and that is outside my control.
  > What this protects you from: Being blamed for an algorithm change. You promise the work, not the follower count.

**Consulting**
- *Advice, Not Guaranteed Results:* My recommendations are based on the information you give me. You decide whether and how to act on them, and I don't guarantee any particular business result.
  > What this protects you from: Being blamed when a client acts on your advice badly, or not at all. They own their decisions.
- *Regulated Advice:* I don't give legal, tax, accounting or investment advice. Where a decision needs one of those, I will suggest you speak to a qualified professional.
  > What this protects you from: Drifting into legal, tax or financial advice you are not licensed to give.

### 17. Termination
**Fixed fee or hourly**
- Flexible: Either of us may end this agreement in writing if the other doesn't fix a serious problem within 14 days of being told about it. You pay for the work done up to that date.
- Standard: Either of us may end this agreement in writing if the other breaks it and doesn't fix the problem within 10 days of written notice. You pay for the work done up to the end date. If you end it for any other reason, the Cancellation & Kill Fee clause applies.
- Firm: Either of us may end this agreement in writing if the other breaks it and doesn't fix the problem within 7 days of written notice. I may also end it if a payment is more than 30 days late. You pay for all work done up to the end date. If you end it for any other reason, the Cancellation & Kill Fee clause applies.

**Monthly retainer**
- Flexible: After the minimum term of [Minimum Months] months ([Minimum Total]), either of us may end this agreement with [Notice Days] days' written notice. You pay for the work done up to the end of the notice period.
- Standard: This retainer has a minimum term of [Minimum Months] months ([Minimum Total]). After that, it continues month to month until either of us ends it with [Notice Days] days' written notice. You pay the monthly fee up to the end of the notice period.
- Firm: This retainer has a minimum term of [Minimum Months] months ([Minimum Total]); if you end it early, the fees for the rest of the minimum term are still due. After that, it continues month to month until either of us ends it with [Notice Days] days' written notice. You pay the monthly fee up to the end of the notice period. I may end it at once if an invoice is more than 30 days late.

> What this protects you from: Being stuck with a client who stopped paying, or being dropped overnight. A clear exit and a notice period give both of you time to plan.

### 18. Governing Law
This agreement is governed by the laws of [Your state/country]. If we disagree, we will first try to settle it by talking in good faith for at least 14 days before taking any formal step.

> What this protects you from: Arguing later about whose rules apply. Ask a local lawyer which state or country to name here.

### 19. Entire Agreement
This agreement, the SOW and any signed Change Orders are the whole agreement between us about this project. They replace earlier emails and conversations. Changes must be in writing and accepted by both of us.

> What this protects you from: “But you said on the call…” Only what is written in these documents counts, and changes have to be written down too.

### 20. Signatures
By signing below, we both agree to this agreement and the Statement of Work dated [Agreement Date]. Each of us keeps a signed copy.

| Freelancer | Client |
|---|---|
| Name (typed): [Freelancer Name], [Freelancer Business] | Name & title (typed): [Client Contact], [Client Title], [Client Company] |
| Signature: ______________________ | Signature: ______________________ |
| Date: ____________ | Date: ____________ |

> What this protects you from: Starting work on a handshake. Typed names and dates on a copy you both keep show what was agreed. This is not an electronic signature: sign by hand or with an e-signature service you trust.
