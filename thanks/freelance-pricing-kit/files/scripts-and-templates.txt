§2  SCOPE OF WORK SECTION (Chapter 3, document 1)
======================================================================
Paste this into your proposal or quote.

Project: [Project name]
Goal: [One sentence on what the finished work should achieve for the client.]

Included
- [Deliverable 1, with a number, e.g. "Design of 5 pages: Home, About, Services, Blog index, Contact"]
- [Deliverable 2, e.g. "Build in WordPress on the client's existing hosting"]
- [Deliverable 3, e.g. "Entry of client-supplied text and images"]
- Revisions: [2] rounds of consolidated feedback per deliverable
- Meetings: [1] kickoff call + [2] check-in calls of up to [30] minutes
- Support: [14] days of bug fixes after launch

Not included (available as add-ons on request)
- [Copywriting or editing of text]
- [Stock photos or paid fonts/plugins, billed at cost if needed]
- [Additional pages beyond the 5 listed]
- [Ongoing maintenance, hosting or updates after the support period]
- [SEO, ads or analytics setup]

Client responsibilities
- Provide [text, images, logins] by [date]
- Send consolidated feedback (one email per round) within [3] working days
- Delays in client materials or feedback move the delivery date by the same amount

Additional work
Anything not listed under "Included" is quoted separately before work starts, at [rate]/hour or as a fixed price agreed in writing through a change order. Rush work (delivery earlier than the agreed timeline) carries a [25]% fee.

Tip: aim for 3-5 "Not included" bullets. Choose the things clients in your field most often assume are included.

How to price a change: estimated hours x (1 + overhead, typically 10-20%) x your rate x rush multiplier (1.0 normal, 1.25 rush, 1.5 urgent), rounded up.
Maya: 4 x 1.15 x 90 x 1.0 = 414, rounded up to the nearest 25 = 425. The Change Order tab in the workbook does this for you.

§6  REPLY SCRIPTS FOR NEW REQUESTS (Chapter 3, document 5)
======================================================================

A. A small favor you absorb
"Done! That one was outside our scope but quick, so no charge this time. For anything else beyond scope I'll send a short quote first."

B. A new feature
"Good idea. It isn't in the current scope, so there are two options: add it now for [price] (delivery moves to [date]), or do it as phase 2 after launch so the current date holds. Which would you prefer? I'll send a change order."

C. An extra revision round
"We've used the [2] included rounds. Further rounds are [price] each. Reply 'go ahead' and I'll start today."

D. The deadline moves earlier
"Here are the options: full scope by [new date] with a [amount] rush fee; core items by [new date] and the rest on the original date at no extra cost; or we keep [date]."

E. A pile-up of small requests
"I've listed the extra requests from the past few weeks: [list]. Together they come to about [X] hours. I suggest one change order for [price]. Once it's approved, I'll fold them into the next delivery."

F. Asked on a call
"Let me check how that fits the scope and come back to you by email today."

The rule behind every script: Never agree to a price or timeline live. Confirm it in writing.

§7  PRICE PUSHBACK REPLIES (Chapter 4)
======================================================================
The one rule: when price comes up, change what's included, never just the price.
Each reply uses Maya's quote (Essential 1,900 / Standard 2,700 / Premium 3,250). Swap in your own packages and amounts.

1. CLIENT: "Can you do it cheaper?"
   DON'T SAY: "I guess I could do 2,200 instead."
   SAY THIS: "The price reflects the work in each package, so I keep it the same. If budget is tight, Essential gets you the core result (refreshed logo and palette) for 1,900."
   Why it works: you offer a cheaper option without suggesting your price was padded.

2. CLIENT: "Another freelancer quoted less."
   DON'T SAY: "Oh, how much? I can match it."
   SAY THIS: "That makes sense. Quotes often cover different things. Mine includes two revision rounds and a mini brand guide. If you send me theirs, I'll tell you honestly where they differ."
   Why it works: it moves the comparison from price to what's included.

3. CLIENT: "We don't have the budget right now."
   DON'T SAY: "No problem, what can you afford? I'll make it work."
   SAY THIS: "Understood. Two options: we start with Essential now for 1,900 and add the social media kit later, or I hold this quote until [date] and we pick it up when the budget is there. Which suits you better?"
   Why it works: both options are a yes, and neither lowers your rate.

4. CLIENT: "Can we start now and pay later?"
   DON'T SAY: "Sure, just pay me when you can."
   SAY THIS: "I start every project with a 50% deposit; for Standard that's 1,350. If cash flow is the issue, I can split the remaining 1,350 into two payments of 675: one when you approve the first concepts and one on delivery."
   Why it works: you stay flexible on timing but never work without a deposit.

5. CLIENT: "Can you do a free sample first?"
   DON'T SAY: "OK, I'll mock up a quick logo idea for you."
   SAY THIS: "I don't do free concept work, but here are [2-3] similar projects with the brief and the result: [links]. If you'd like a smaller first step, Essential starts with a 950 deposit."
   Why it works: past work is the proof, and a small paid step replaces free work.

6. CLIENT: "We'll have lots more work for you later."
   DON'T SAY: "Great, then I'll do this first one at a discount."
   SAY THIS: "Great to hear, I'd like that too. I price each project on its own. Once we've done a few together, I'm happy to talk about a monthly retainer with priority time."
   Why it works: future work gets a real agreement later, not a discount now for a promise.

7. CLIENT: "If we book six months of work, can we get a discount?"
   DON'T SAY: "Sure, let's say 25% off everything."
   SAY THIS: "For a fixed six-month commitment, paid monthly in advance, I can take 5% off the monthly fee. Booked time saves me sales and admin work, so I pass part of that on. Without the commitment, my standard rates apply."
   Why it works: the discount buys you something real: guaranteed, prepaid work.

8. CLIENT: "Can you send an hourly breakdown?"
   DON'T SAY: "Sure: 2 hours discovery, 12 hours logo, 4 hours revisions..."
   SAY THIS: "I price by package, not by the hour, so you know the total up front and it doesn't grow if a task takes longer. Here's what each package delivers: [list]. If one item isn't needed, tell me and I'll quote a version without it."
   Why it works: you talk about deliverables, not hours to haggle over, and removing scope stays possible.

Before you reply to any of these: reply in writing, not live on a call (script F in §6). Keep the reply short: one sentence on why the price stays, one option that changes the scope, one question that moves things forward.

Nothing changes for the work we already have planned. Thank you for being one of my first clients.

Best,
[Your name]

--- §8C  Email C: retainer or regular client ---

Subject: Your monthly fee from [April 1]

Hi [Name],

A heads-up on fees: from [April 1], the monthly fee for [4 articles a month] will be [1,750] (currently [1,500]). The work stays the same: [same deliverables, same turnaround, same monthly call].

If you'd rather keep the fee where it is, we can move to [3 articles a month] at [1,500] instead. Just reply with which you prefer by [March 15]. Thanks for [two years] of good work together.

Best,
[Your name]

Maya's numbers (Rate Increase tab): 90 -> 100, 88 billable hours a month, expected loss 5% of client hours.
Current monthly revenue 90 x 88 = 7,920. New monthly revenue 100 x 83.6 = 8,360 (+440).
Break-even client loss = 1 - 90 / 100 = 10%.

12-month cash-flow stress test (pricing-calculator.html, Step 5):
Maya loses 15% of client hours (13.2 h) and refills them evenly over 4 months (3.3 h a month) at 100.
Hours in month m = kept hours (74.8) + 3.3 x (m - 1), capped at 88.
Month 1: 74.8 h, 7,480 (-440, running -440)
Month 2: 78.1 h, 7,810 (-110, running -550)
Month 3: 81.4 h, 8,140 (+220, running -330)
Month 4: 84.7 h, 8,470 (+550, running +220)
Month 5: 88.0 h, 8,800 (+880, running +1,100)
Months 6 to 12: 8,800 (+880 each), running total +7,260 after 12 months.
Cash cushion needed: 550. Worst case with no refill: -440 a month, -5,280 over 12 months.

§9  REPLIES WHEN CLIENTS RESPOND TO A RATE INCREASE (Chapter 5)
======================================================================

1. CLIENT: "That's more than we budgeted for."
   SAY THIS: "Understood. Two options: book the next project before [February 1] at the current rate, or we adjust the scope so it fits your budget at the new rate. I'm happy to suggest what to trim."

2. CLIENT: "Can you keep our old rate?"
   SAY THIS: "I value working with you, which is why I'm giving you notice and keeping your current rate until [date]. After that, the new rate applies to all my clients, so it stays fair for everyone."

3. CLIENT: "We'll have to find someone cheaper."
   SAY THIS: "I understand, and thanks for telling me. If it helps the handover, I'll send the final files and my notes from our projects. If you'd like to work together again later, my door's open."

4. CLIENT: "Can you phase it in?"
   SAY THIS: "Yes. The rate goes to [95] from [February 1] and to [100] from [May 1]. Both dates are fixed, so you can budget for them now."

5. CLIENT: "Will you lock our rate if we sign for a year?"
   SAY THIS: "Yes. With a 12-month agreement, paid monthly in advance, I'll hold [100] for the whole term. Without it, I review rates once a year with [30] days' notice."

6. CLIENT: (No reply after a week.)
   SAY THIS: "Hi [Name], just checking that my note about rates from [February 1] reached you. You don't need to do anything unless you'd like to book work at the current rate before then. Happy to answer any questions."

Stick to it: once the email is sent, don't negotiate the rate itself. Change the scope, the timing or the package, the same way as in Chapter 4.

§10  MONTHLY RETAINER OFFER EMAIL (Chapter 6)
======================================================================

Subject: A simpler way to work together each month

Hi [Name],

Since we're doing [about 4 posts] a month, I'd like to offer a fixed monthly package instead of per-word invoices: [4 posts a month], research, one round of edits and a [30]-minute planning call, for [1,750] a month, invoiced on the 1st. It runs for [3] months to start, then month to month with [30] days' notice.

You get a fixed budget and guaranteed slots in my calendar. Shall I send the agreement?

Best,
[Your name]

--- The math behind it (Theo, fictional content writer, floor rate 70) ---

Per-word to hourly:
  effective hourly = price per piece / total hours per piece
  minimum per piece = total hours x floor rate
  minimum per word = minimum per piece / words
Theo: 1,500 words x 0.12 = 180 per post; 6 hours per post (research 1.5, draft 3, edits 1, admin 0.5).
  180 / 6 = 30 per hour. At his floor: 6 x 70 = 420 per post = 0.28 per word.

Monthly retainer:
  monthly hours = pieces x hours per piece + fixed monthly hours (planning call, reporting)
  monthly fee = monthly hours x rate x (1 - commitment discount), rounded up to 50
Theo (6 h per post, 2 fixed hours, rate 70, 5% off for a 3-month minimum paid in advance):
  Starter, 2 posts: 14 h x 70 = 980, -5% = 931 -> 950 a month (475 per post)
  Growth, 4 posts:  26 h x 70 = 1,820, -5% = 1,729 -> 1,750 a month (437.50 per post)
  Full, 6 posts:    38 h x 70 = 2,660, -5% = 2,527 -> 2,550 a month (425 per post)

Retainer terms to write down:
- Billing: invoiced on the 1st, paid before the month's work starts.
- Term: 3-month minimum, then [30] days' notice from either side.
- Unused pieces: roll over one month at most, then expire.
- Extra pieces: 420 each (6 h x 70), quoted before work starts.
- Rate review: once a year, with [30] days' notice (Email C, §8C).
