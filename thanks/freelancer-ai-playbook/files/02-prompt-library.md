# 02 – Prompt Library
### 55 copy-paste prompts for every part of your freelance business

---

## How to Use This Library

Each prompt has a **[BRACKET]** for things you need to fill in. Some prompts have optional additions marked with *(optional)*. 

Before using any prompt, paste your context document (see 01 – AI Foundations) at the top of the conversation. Then use the prompt. You'll get dramatically better results.

---

## Section A: Client Discovery & Outreach (8 prompts)

---

**A1 – Research a prospect**
```
I'm a freelance [YOUR SPECIALTY] and I'm about to reach out to [COMPANY NAME], a [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]. 

Research their likely pain points, what they value in vendors, and what I should know before contacting them. Focus on what's relevant to my services. Format as a bulleted brief I can review in 3 minutes.
```

---

**A2 – Write a cold outreach email**
```
Write a short, non-spammy cold outreach email from me to [NAME], [TITLE] at [COMPANY].

Context:
- My specialty: [YOUR SPECIALTY]
- Why I'm reaching out to them specifically: [REASON — be specific, e.g., "I saw their recent rebrand and noticed the website copy hasn't been updated"]
- One relevant result I've achieved for a similar client: [RESULT]
- Desired outcome: a 20-minute call

Rules: no buzzwords, no flattery, no "I hope this email finds you well." Keep it under 120 words. Sound like a human, not a marketing email.
```

---

**A3 – Follow-up email (no response)**
```
I sent a cold outreach email to [NAME] at [COMPANY] [X DAYS] ago and haven't heard back.

Original email: [PASTE EMAIL]

Write a short, non-pushy follow-up. Acknowledge I haven't heard back, add one new piece of value or context (suggest something based on the original email), and make it easy to respond yes or no. Under 80 words.
```

---

**A4 – LinkedIn connection request**
```
Write a LinkedIn connection request to [NAME], [TITLE] at [COMPANY].

Context: [BRIEF REASON YOU'RE CONNECTING — e.g., "we met at a conference," "I admire their work on X," "I'm in the same niche"]

Rules: under 300 characters, no selling, no generic "I'd like to add you to my network." Should feel genuine and specific.
```

---

**A5 – Profile positioning statement**
```
Help me write a positioning statement for my freelance profile (LinkedIn headline or website tagline).

My specialty: [SPECIALTY]
The type of client I serve best: [CLIENT TYPE]
The main result I help them achieve: [RESULT]
My differentiator vs. other freelancers: [WHAT MAKES YOU DIFFERENT]

Write 5 variations from 10 to 25 words. Avoid jargon. Focus on outcomes, not activities.
```

---

**A6 – Case study outline**
```
I want to write a case study about a project I completed. Help me outline it.

Client type: [TYPE — no names if confidential]
Problem they had: [PROBLEM]
What I did: [YOUR WORK]
Results achieved: [RESULTS — be specific with numbers if possible]

Create a 5-section case study outline (situation, challenge, approach, results, takeaway) with 2–3 bullet points per section I should address. Keep it skimmable.
```

---

**A7 – Referral request message**
```
Write a brief, natural-sounding message asking a past client for referrals.

Relationship context: [DESCRIBE THE PROJECT AND HOW IT WENT]
What kind of referral I'm looking for: [DESCRIBE IDEAL CLIENT]

Rules: don't be transactional. Make it easy to say yes or no. Under 100 words. Should feel like a genuine request, not a sales script.
```

---

**A8 – Niche identification exercise**
```
I'm a freelance [BROAD CATEGORY — e.g., "graphic designer" or "marketing consultant"].

Here's what I've worked on in the past: [LIST 5-10 PROJECTS OR CLIENTS]
Here's what I most enjoyed: [DESCRIBE]
Here's where I got the best results: [DESCRIBE]

Based on this, suggest 5 specific niches I could position myself in. For each, describe: (1) who the client is, (2) what specific problem I'd solve, (3) why I'd be competitive in that niche, (4) estimated price range for a typical project.
```

## Section B: Scoping & Proposals (8 prompts)

---

**B1 – Discovery call prep**
```
I have a discovery call with [CLIENT DESCRIPTION] in [X HOURS/DAYS]. They want [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF WHAT THEY'VE TOLD ME].

Generate 10 discovery questions I should ask to:
1. Fully understand the problem and its business impact
2. Understand their definition of success
3. Surface any red flags or scope risks
4. Understand their timeline, budget range, and decision process

Organize by priority — most important first.
```

---

**B2 – Scope of work draft**
```
Write a clear scope of work section for a freelance proposal.

Project: [PROJECT DESCRIPTION]
What's included: [LIST DELIVERABLES]
What's NOT included (important): [LIST EXCLUSIONS]
Number of revisions: [NUMBER]
My role vs. client's role: [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU EACH DO]

Format as a professional SOW section I can paste into a proposal document. Use clear, plain language. Include a "Not Included" section explicitly.
```

---

**B3 – Proposal email (initial send)**
```
Write a short email to send alongside a proposal document.

Context:
- Client: [CLIENT DESCRIPTION]
- Project: [PROJECT SUMMARY]
- Proposal highlights: [2-3 KEY POINTS FROM YOUR PROPOSAL]
- Next step you want: [E.G., "review and reply with questions" or "sign by Friday"]

Rules: under 150 words. Professional but warm. Don't summarize the whole proposal — tease the value and drive the action.
```

---

**B4 – Handle a scope creep conversation**
```
A client is asking me to [DESCRIBE THE EXTRA REQUEST]. This is outside our original scope, which covered [ORIGINAL SCOPE].

Write a professional, non-confrontational response that:
1. Acknowledges the request positively
2. Notes it's outside our original scope
3. Offers to add it for [PRICE] or in a future phase
4. Keeps the relationship intact

Tone: collaborative, not defensive. Under 150 words.
```

---

**B5 – Project risk assessment**
```
I'm about to start a project: [PROJECT DESCRIPTION]
Client: [CLIENT TYPE]
Timeline: [TIMELINE]
Budget: [BUDGET]

List the 5 most likely risks that could derail this project, with a brief description of each and a simple mitigation step I could take proactively.
```

---

**B6 – Project brief template**
```
Create a project brief template I can use to onboard [TYPE OF PROJECT — e.g., "website copywriting" or "brand strategy"] clients.

It should capture: project goals, target audience, competitors, tone/style preferences, key messages, deliverables, timeline, approval process, and any brand assets needed.

Format as a fillable document with short explanatory notes for each field. The client should be able to complete it in 20 minutes.
```

---

**B7 – Rate justification script**
```
A potential client pushed back on my rate of [YOUR RATE], saying it's higher than what they expected.

My rate is justified because: [2-3 REASONS — experience, results, specialization]

Write a confident, non-defensive response that:
1. Acknowledges their concern
2. Reframes the rate in terms of value (ROI), not hours
3. Offers a clear path forward (either they accept, or you adjust scope to fit their budget)

Under 150 words. No apologizing for the rate.
```

---

**B8 – Project kickoff agenda**
```
Write a kickoff meeting agenda for a [PROJECT TYPE] project.

Participants: me (freelancer) and [CLIENT DESCRIPTION]
Duration: [X MINUTES]
Project goals: [GOALS]

The agenda should cover: introductions/rapport, project goals alignment, working process and communication norms, timeline and milestones, immediate next steps (who does what before next meeting). Format as a clean agenda I can share with the client in advance.
```

## Section C: Doing the Work (10 prompts)

---

**C1 – Research briefing**
```
I need to research [TOPIC] for a client project. The client is [CLIENT DESCRIPTION] and they need this information to [PURPOSE].

Give me:
1. The 5 most important questions I should answer through my research
2. The most reliable types of sources for each question
3. A suggested research structure (what to tackle first, second, etc.)

Don't do the research yet — just help me plan it efficiently.
```

---

**C2 – First draft from notes**
```
I have rough notes from [MEETING/RESEARCH/INTERVIEW]. Turn them into a coherent first draft of [DELIVERABLE — e.g., "an executive summary," "a strategy memo," "a website page"].

Notes: [PASTE YOUR NOTES]

Target audience: [AUDIENCE]
Tone: [TONE]
Length: [APPROXIMATE LENGTH]

Keep my key points but organize them logically, fill in transitions, and make it readable. I'll edit heavily — give me a solid structural draft.
```

---

**C3 – Improve a draft**
```
Here's a draft I've written: [PASTE DRAFT]

Please:
1. Identify the 3 biggest weaknesses (clarity, logic, tone, structure, etc.)
2. Rewrite the weakest section to show how to improve it
3. Give me a list of specific edits to make to the rest

Don't rewrite the whole thing unless I ask — I want to understand the problems and fix them myself.
```

---

**C4 – Create an outline**
```
I need to write [DELIVERABLE — e.g., "a 2,000-word industry report" / "a 10-slide presentation" / "a 5-email onboarding sequence"].

Topic: [TOPIC]
Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Goal (what should they think/feel/do after): [GOAL]
Key points I need to cover: [LIST]

Create a detailed outline with section headers and 2–3 bullet points per section describing what each part should accomplish.
```

---

**C5 – Simplify complex content**
```
Here's a complex piece of content: [PASTE CONTENT]

Rewrite it so a [TARGET AUDIENCE — e.g., "small business owner with no technical background"] can understand it in one read. 

Rules: no jargon without explanation, use short sentences and paragraphs, use concrete examples where helpful. Target reading level: [GRADE LEVEL, e.g., "8th grade"].
```

---

**C6 – Generate alternatives**
```
I've written this: [PASTE CONTENT — e.g., a headline, tagline, section, paragraph]

Generate 8 alternative versions that vary in:
- Tone (e.g., more direct, more conversational, more authoritative)
- Approach (e.g., problem-first, solution-first, curiosity-driven)
- Length (short, medium, and long variations)

Label each version with its approach so I can compare easily.
```

---

**C7 – Summarize research**
```
Here is research I've collected on [TOPIC]: [PASTE RESEARCH OR KEY FINDINGS]

Summarize this into:
1. A 5-bullet executive summary (what matters most)
2. 3 key takeaways relevant to [CLIENT/PROJECT CONTEXT]
3. 2 open questions this research doesn't answer

Keep it tight. I'm using this to brief a client, not publish it.
```

---

**C8 – Review for consistency**
```
Here are two pieces of content that should match in tone and style: 

Piece 1: [PASTE]
Piece 2: [PASTE]

List every inconsistency you find: tone differences, terminology differences, formatting differences, contradictions. Then suggest specific fixes.
```

---

**C9 – Create a checklist**
```
Create a quality-control checklist for [DELIVERABLE TYPE — e.g., "a brand strategy document" / "a website copywriting project" / "a marketing campaign"].

The checklist should cover: completeness (is everything there?), quality (is it good?), consistency (does it align with brief?), client-readiness (can it be sent as-is?).

Format as a checkbox list I can run through before every delivery. Include 15–25 items.
```

---

**C10 – Translate jargon for clients**
```
My client doesn't have a background in [YOUR FIELD]. I need to explain [CONCEPT OR PROCESS] to them.

Write a plain-English explanation suitable for a non-expert. Use an analogy if helpful. Anticipate the 2–3 follow-up questions they're most likely to ask, and answer those too. Aim for a 1-minute read.
```

## Section D: Client Communication (10 prompts)

---

**D1 – Project status update**
```
Write a weekly status update email to a client.

Project: [PROJECT NAME]
What I completed this week: [LIST]
What's in progress: [LIST]
What I need from the client: [LIST — if anything]
Any risks or changes to flag: [OR "none"]
Next milestone: [MILESTONE AND DATE]

Format: clear, professional, under 200 words. Use bullet points. Start with a one-line summary of where we stand.
```

---

**D2 – Deliver a project**
```
Write an email to deliver [DELIVERABLE] to a client.

Context: [BRIEF PROJECT CONTEXT]
What I'm delivering: [DESCRIBE]
Key decisions or trade-offs I made: [1-2 THINGS TO CALL OUT]
What I need from them: [FEEDBACK? APPROVAL? PAYMENT?]
Deadline for response (if any): [DATE OR "none"]

Tone: confident and warm. I'm proud of this work. Under 150 words.
```

---

**D3 – Handle a critical client**
```
A client gave me critical or negative feedback: "[PASTE THEIR FEEDBACK]"

Write a professional response that:
1. Thanks them for the feedback without being sycophantic
2. Shows I understood their concern (restate it briefly)
3. Explains my original thinking briefly (not defensively)
4. Proposes a concrete next step to resolve it

Tone: calm, collaborative, professional. Not defensive. Not a pushover. Under 150 words.
```

---

**D4 – Reschedule or delay**
```
I need to tell a client that [DESCRIBE THE DELAY OR CHANGE — e.g., "a deliverable will be 3 days late" / "I need to reschedule our meeting"].

Reason: [HONEST REASON — keep it brief]
What I'm doing to address it: [YOUR MITIGATION PLAN]
New date/timeline: [NEW DATE]

Write a short, direct message that owns the situation without over-explaining. No excuses, just facts and a clear path forward. Under 100 words.
```

---

**D5 – Say no to a client request**
```
A client is asking me to [DESCRIBE REQUEST]. I need to decline because [YOUR REASON — e.g., "it's outside my expertise," "it would compromise the project," "I don't have bandwidth"].

Write a professional, warm refusal that:
1. Declines clearly (no ambiguity)
2. Explains briefly (one sentence)
3. Offers an alternative where possible
4. Keeps the relationship positive

Under 100 words.
```

---

**D6 – Close out a project**
```
A project is wrapping up. Write a closing email to the client.

Project summary: [WHAT WE DID]
Results/outcome: [WHAT WAS ACHIEVED]
What they're receiving: [FINAL DELIVERABLES]
Any handoff notes: [THINGS THEY NEED TO KNOW GOING FORWARD]
Optional: open the door to future work

Tone: warm, professional, proud of the work. Under 200 words.
```

---

**D7 – Ask for a testimonial**
```
A project just ended well. Write a short message asking the client for a testimonial.

Context: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF PROJECT AND OUTCOME]
Where I'll use it: [E.G., "my website and LinkedIn"]
Make it easy: offer to write a draft for them to edit *(optional — include this if you want)*

Under 80 words. Feels like a human ask, not a template.
```

---

**D8 – Raise rates with existing client**
```
I want to raise my rates for a recurring client from [OLD RATE] to [NEW RATE], effective [DATE].

Context:
- How long I've worked with them: [TIME]
- Value I've delivered: [BRIEF SUMMARY]
- Reason for increase: [YOUR REASON — e.g., "market adjustment," "increased expertise," "demand for my work"]

Write a professional, direct message announcing the change that treats them like an adult and doesn't over-apologize. Under 150 words.
```

---

**D9 – Difficult conversation opener**
```
I need to have a difficult conversation with a client about [DESCRIBE SITUATION — e.g., "they've been unresponsive for 3 weeks," "they're repeatedly missing deadlines on their end," "I think the project is heading in the wrong direction"].

Write an opening message that:
1. Opens the conversation without blame
2. States my concern clearly and factually
3. Invites their perspective
4. Suggests a specific solution or next step

Tone: direct but collaborative. Not passive-aggressive. Not confrontational. Under 150 words.
```

---

**D10 – Invoice reminder (overdue)**
```
Write a follow-up message for an overdue invoice.

Invoice amount: [AMOUNT]
Original due date: [DATE]
Days overdue: [NUMBER]
This is my [FIRST / SECOND / THIRD] follow-up

Tone: professional and firm. First follow-up = assume it slipped through. Second = clear expectation. Third = serious but not hostile. Under 100 words each version.

Write one version appropriate for where I am in the sequence.
```

## Section E: Business Development (10 prompts)

---

**E1 – Content idea generation**
```
I want to create content (LinkedIn posts, a newsletter, or short articles) to attract [IDEAL CLIENT TYPE] clients.

My specialty: [SPECIALTY]
What my ideal clients struggle with: [PAIN POINTS]
What I know that they don't: [YOUR EXPERTISE]

Generate 15 specific content ideas. For each, give: topic, angle (why it's interesting), and the one key insight or takeaway. Prioritize ideas that demonstrate expertise, not just awareness.
```

---

**E2 – LinkedIn post draft**
```
Write a LinkedIn post about [TOPIC].

My audience: [AUDIENCE — e.g., "marketing directors at B2B tech companies"]
The main insight or story: [DESCRIBE]
What I want them to take away: [TAKEAWAY]

Format: hook in the first line (no "I'm excited to share" openers), short paragraphs, one concrete insight, end with a question or call to action. Under 250 words. Sound like a knowledgeable human, not a content marketer.
```

---

**E3 – Newsletter issue outline**
```
I write a [FREQUENCY] newsletter for [AUDIENCE — e.g., "freelance consultants"].

Topic for this issue: [TOPIC]
Key point I want to make: [POINT]
Personal story or example I can include: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OR "none"]

Outline a newsletter issue with: subject line (5 options), intro hook, main content structure (3–4 sections), and a call to action. Keep the outline tight — I'm writing the content, just need the structure.
```

---

**E4 – Define ideal client profile**
```
Help me define my ideal client profile (ICP).

My service: [SERVICE]
Clients I've loved working with: [DESCRIBE 2-3]
Clients I've disliked: [DESCRIBE 1-2 WITHOUT NAMING]
My best results came from: [DESCRIBE SITUATION]

Based on this, describe my ICP across these dimensions: industry, company size, role/title, situation they're in (what triggers them to hire someone like me), what they value in a vendor, red flags to watch for.
```

---

**E5 – Partnership pitch**
```
I want to propose a referral or collaboration partnership to [PERSON/COMPANY — e.g., "a web designer who works with my same clients"].

What I can offer them: [WHAT YOU BRING]
What they could offer me: [WHAT THEY BRING]
Why it benefits their clients: [CLIENT BENEFIT]

Write a short message proposing the partnership. Under 150 words. Feel like a genuine suggestion, not a pitch deck.
```

---

**E6 – Analyze a competitor's positioning**
```
Here's a competitor's website copy or bio: [PASTE THEIR TEXT]

Analyze:
1. How they're positioning themselves
2. The clients they seem to target
3. Their key differentiators
4. What they don't address or a gap in their positioning
5. One thing I could learn from them, and one thing I could do differently

Be specific and honest, not cheerleading.
```

---

**E7 – Price anchoring strategy**
```
I offer [YOUR SERVICE] and currently charge [YOUR RATE].

Help me design a 3-tier pricing structure that:
1. Makes my target price feel like the obvious middle choice
2. Has a genuine lower option (smaller scope, not just lower price)
3. Has a genuine premium option with real added value
4. Makes it easy for clients to say yes

For each tier, describe the scope, the price logic, and who it's for.
```

---

**E8 – Annual business review prompts**
```
I want to do an annual review of my freelance business. Give me 15 specific questions to reflect on across these areas: revenue and clients (what worked, what didn't), skills and development, work quality and satisfaction, systems and efficiency, and goals for next year.

Each question should prompt honest reflection, not just summary. Include at least 2 uncomfortable questions.
```

---

**E9 – Waitlist/limited availability message**
```
I'm fully booked until [DATE] and want to let prospects know without losing them.

Write a short, professional message for my website or email that:
1. States my current availability honestly
2. Creates some urgency (limited spots)
3. Makes it easy to get on my list for when I'm available
4. Doesn't sound fake-busy or manipulative

Under 100 words.
```

---

**E10 – 90-day growth plan**
```
I want to grow my freelance income by [GOAL — e.g., "25%"] over the next 90 days.

Current situation:
- Services: [YOUR SERVICES]
- Current revenue: [APPROXIMATE]
- Main bottleneck (your honest read): [BOTTLENECK — e.g., "not enough leads," "low conversion," "too slow delivery," "low rates"]

Give me a concrete 90-day plan with specific actions by month. Focus on the highest-leverage moves for my specific bottleneck. Be realistic, not motivational.
```

## Section F: Admin & Operations (9 prompts)

---

**F1 – Invoice draft**
```
Write the line items for an invoice for the following project:

Client: [CLIENT TYPE — no names if needed]
Project: [PROJECT DESCRIPTION]
What was delivered: [LIST OF DELIVERABLES]
Total agreed price: [PRICE]
Payment terms: [E.G., "net 15," "due on receipt"]
Late fee policy: [OR "none"]

Format as a professional invoice line-item structure I can paste into my invoicing tool. Include a brief project description at the top.
```

---

**F2 – Contract clause review**
```
Here is a contract clause a client sent me: [PASTE CLAUSE]

Explain in plain English:
1. What this clause means
2. Whether it's standard or unusual
3. Any risk it poses to me as a freelancer
4. Whether I should push back, and what I could ask for instead

Note: this is informational only — I'll consult a lawyer for anything significant.
```

---

**F3 – End-of-year tax prep checklist**
```
Create an end-of-year tax preparation checklist for a self-employed freelancer in [COUNTRY/REGION].

Include: income documentation, expense categories to review, deductions commonly missed by freelancers, things to do before December 31st vs. things that can wait until filing, and a list of documents to have ready for an accountant.

Note: this is a general checklist, not legal or tax advice.
```

---

**F4 – Project retrospective**
```
A project just ended. Help me run a quick retrospective.

Project: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
What went well: [YOUR NOTES]
What didn't go well: [YOUR NOTES]
What I'd do differently: [YOUR INITIAL THOUGHTS]

Organize my notes into a structured retrospective summary with: 3 wins to build on, 3 lessons learned, and 3 specific changes to make to my process for the next similar project.
```

---

**F5 – Draft a client contract (framework)**
```
Create a plain-English freelance services agreement framework for [TYPE OF SERVICE — e.g., "copywriting" / "web design" / "consulting"].

Include sections for: scope of work (placeholder), payment terms, revision policy, intellectual property/ownership, confidentiality, kill fee, late payment, and termination.

Write each section in clear, non-legalese language. Flag which sections I should have a lawyer review before using with real clients.
```

---

**F6 – Expense categorization help**
```
Here is a list of my business expenses from this [MONTH/QUARTER]: [PASTE LIST]

Categorize each into standard freelance business expense categories (e.g., software/subscriptions, professional development, marketing, equipment, home office, travel, etc.). 

Flag any that might not be fully deductible and explain why. Note: I'll verify with my accountant.
```

---

**F7 – Set up a client portal structure**
```
I want to set up a simple client portal using [TOOL — e.g., Notion, Google Drive, ClickUp].

Type of work I do: [YOUR SERVICE]
Typical project duration: [DURATION]
What clients most need: [E.G., "to see progress, access deliverables, give feedback"]

Design a folder/page structure for the portal that covers: project overview, shared files, communication log, feedback rounds, invoicing, and final delivery. Keep it simple enough that clients actually use it.
```

---

**F8 – Automate a repetitive process**
```
I repeatedly do this task in my business: [DESCRIBE TASK — e.g., "write the same type of onboarding email for every new client," "create the same report structure every month"]

What are the steps I always follow? [LIST THE STEPS]

Help me create a repeatable template or system for this so I never build it from scratch again. Include: a template I can reuse, the 3–4 things I need to customize each time, and any parts that AI could help draft for me.
```

---

**F9 – Capacity planning**
```
Here is my current project load:
[LIST YOUR ACTIVE PROJECTS AND ESTIMATED HOURS PER WEEK]

My target working hours per week: [HOURS]
Buffer I want to keep for admin, business development, and surprises: [HOURS OR PERCENTAGE]

Calculate how much capacity I have for new work. If I'm over capacity, suggest which project types to defer or which tasks to batch. If I have capacity, suggest what type of new project would fit.
```
