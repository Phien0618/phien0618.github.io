# 03 – Client Workflows
### AI-powered processes for every phase of client work

---

## Overview

This section gives you a repeatable process for each phase of client work. Each workflow tells you: what to do, which AI prompts to use (referencing the prompt library), and what the output looks like.

Work through each workflow once on a real project. After that, it becomes muscle memory.

---

## Workflow 1: Pre-Discovery (Before the First Call)

**Time needed:** 20–30 minutes  
**When to use:** As soon as a lead confirms a discovery call

### Steps

**Step 1 – Research the company (10 min)**  
Use prompt **A1** to build a quick brief on the company. Feed it anything public you've found: their website, LinkedIn, any news. Your goal: know their context, likely pain points, and relevant vocabulary before the call.

**Step 2 – Prepare discovery questions (10 min)**  
Use prompt **B1** with the context from Step 1. Print or keep open during the call.

**Step 3 – Set your intention (2 min)**  
Write down one sentence: "After this call, I will know whether [CONDITION — e.g., 'this is a fit for my services and what a project scope would look like']."

This forces you to be outcome-focused, not just conversational.

**Output:** A 1-page pre-call brief with company context + your discovery questions.

## Workflow 2: Scoping & Proposal

**Time needed:** 1–3 hours  
**When to use:** After a successful discovery call

### Steps

**Step 1 – Debrief the discovery call (15 min)**  
Immediately after the call, write a rough brain-dump of notes: what they said, what you noticed, what you're not sure about, what you'd need to do the project.

**Step 2 – Identify risks (10 min)**  
Use prompt **B5** with your notes. Take any risks seriously — better to flag them now than to live with them for 3 months.

**Step 3 – Draft the scope of work (30–60 min)**  
Use prompt **B2**. The key discipline here: be specific about what's NOT included. This is where projects blow up. Review the output carefully and add your own exclusions.

**Step 4 – Price the project**  
Use the rate-setting worksheet in file 04. Don't skip this step — guessing rates leads to resentment.

**Step 5 – Write the proposal**  
Structure:
1. **The situation** — their problem in your words (shows you listened)
2. **The goal** — what success looks like
3. **My approach** — how you'll work, your process, key decisions
4. **What's included** — your SOW (from Step 3)
5. **Investment** — your fee (with tiered options if relevant, see prompt E7)
6. **Timeline** — key milestones
7. **Next steps** — what they need to do

Use prompt **C4** to outline each section, then write the content yourself.

**Step 6 – Send the proposal email**  
Use prompt **B3** to write the covering email.

**Output:** A complete proposal document + covering email, ready to send.

## Workflow 3: Client Onboarding

**Time needed:** 1–2 hours  
**When to use:** After the client signs and pays the first invoice

### Steps

**Step 1 – Create the project brief**  
Use prompt **B6** to generate a brief template for your type of work. Send it to the client with a 5-business-day deadline to complete it. Make clear that the project timeline starts when you receive it.

**Step 2 – Set up the client portal**  
Use prompt **F7** to design the folder/page structure. Keep it simple: most clients won't use anything complex.

**Step 3 – Schedule the kickoff meeting**  
Use prompt **B8** to create the agenda. Send it 24 hours in advance.

**Step 4 – Run the kickoff**  
Stick to the agenda. The most important outcome: alignment on what "done" looks like and who's responsible for what. Write it down and send a follow-up summary within 2 hours of the call.

**Step 5 – Send the kickoff summary**  
Immediately after: paste your notes into AI with this instruction:
> "Turn these rough notes into a clean kickoff meeting summary with: decisions made, next steps (with owner and deadline), and open questions. Bullet points, under 300 words."

Send to the client. This builds confidence and creates a written record.

**Output:** Completed brief, project portal, kickoff summary.

## Workflow 4: Project Execution

**Time needed:** Ongoing  
**When to use:** Throughout the project

### Weekly rhythm

**Monday:** Review the project brief and your deliverables list. Write your 3 priorities for the week. Note anything you need from the client this week.

**Daily work:**  
- Use prompts **C1–C10** as needed for research, drafting, reviewing, and simplifying.
- Start with an outline (C4) before drafting anything substantial.
- Run your drafts through a self-review before client delivery.

**Friday (or end of your work week):**  
- Send a status update using prompt **D1**.
- Note anything that changed from the original scope.
- Flag risks or delays early — always better to tell clients before a deadline than after.

**Before any delivery:**  
Run the quality-control checklist from prompt **C9**. If you don't have one for your deliverable type yet, create one and save it.

### Managing scope creep

Scope creep is normal. The question is how you handle it.

When a client asks for something outside scope:
1. Don't say "no" — say "yes, and here's what that looks like."
2. Use prompt **B4** to draft the response.
3. Decide whether to add it to the current project (for a fee) or queue it for a follow-on project.

The rule: **every time you do out-of-scope work for free, you train the client to ask for more.**

## Workflow 5: Delivery & Close

**Time needed:** 1–2 hours  
**When to use:** When the project is complete

### Steps

**Step 1 – Pre-delivery review (30 min)**  
Run the QC checklist (C9). Review the original brief: did you deliver everything? Does it match what they asked for? Fix anything before sending.

**Step 2 – Write the delivery email**  
Use prompt **D2**. Call out 1–2 key decisions you made and why. This shows expertise and makes the client feel confident in the work.

**Step 3 – Manage the feedback round**  
Set a clear deadline for feedback (5–7 business days is standard). After that, if the project needs further rounds, specify the revision policy.

When feedback comes in:
- If constructive: use prompt **D3** to respond, make the changes, re-deliver.
- If unclear: ask clarifying questions before doing any work.
- If outside scope: treat like scope creep (prompt B4).

**Step 4 – Close the project**  
Use prompt **D6** for the closing email. Include any handoff notes (logins, files, next steps they own).

**Step 5 – Ask for a testimonial**  
Use prompt **D7**. Do this within 48 hours of closing — it's when enthusiasm is highest.

**Step 6 – Invoice final payment**  
Send the final invoice using prompt **F1** and add a note thanking them for the project. Don't let final invoices sit — send immediately.

**Step 7 – Run your retrospective (15 min)**  
Use prompt **F4** within one week. This is how you improve with every project.

**Output:** Final delivery, testimonial request, invoice, retrospective notes.

## Workflow 6: Re-engagement & Referrals

**Time needed:** 30–60 minutes per quarter  
**When to use:** 3–6 months after project completion, and quarterly thereafter

### Steps

**Step 1 – Client audit**  
Quarterly, review your past clients. For each: did we get a good result? Would I work with them again? Are they the type to refer others?

**Step 2 – Re-engagement messages**  
For your top 5 past clients, send a short check-in (not a sales pitch). Something like:
> "Hey [Name] — just thinking about [PROJECT]. Hope things are going well with [OUTCOME]. I'm working on a few projects in your space and thought of you. Any big challenges on your plate I might be able to help with?"

Use prompt **A7** to craft referral requests for your best-fit clients.

**Step 3 – Referral program (simple)**  
If you don't have one, add one line to your closing emails:
> "I grow almost entirely through referrals. If you know someone who could use [YOUR SERVICE], I'd be grateful for an introduction."

That's it. Many happy clients will refer when simply asked — try asking before building an incentive scheme.

**Output:** A short list of re-engagement messages sent, plus a referral touchpoint added to your closing workflow.
