# 04 – Proposals & Rate Templates
### A winning proposal structure + a rate-setting worksheet

---

## Part 1: The Rate-Setting Worksheet

Many freelancers undercharge, usually because rates are set by guessing what "feels right" or matching what they've seen others charge. This worksheet replaces guessing with math.

Work through all four methods, then use the result to make a confident decision.

---

### Method 1: The Minimum Viable Rate

**Step 1 – Calculate your monthly expenses**

| Category | Monthly Amount |
|---|---|
| Housing (rent/mortgage) | $________ |
| Food | $________ |
| Transportation | $________ |
| Health insurance | $________ |
| Business software/tools | $________ |
| Professional development | $________ |
| Taxes set-aside (check your local rate; often 25–35% of income) | $________ |
| Savings/retirement target | $________ |
| Everything else | $________ |
| **TOTAL MONTHLY NEED** | **$________** |

**Step 2 – Calculate your billable hours**

Working days per month (typically 20–22): ________  
Hours you can realistically bill per day (note: not all work hours are billable): ________  
**Billable hours per month:** ________

Example: if you work 40 hrs/week and about 55% of that is billable, 40 × 0.55 × 4.3 weeks ≈ **94 billable hours/month**. Use your own number.

**Step 3 – Minimum hourly rate**

Minimum hourly rate = Total Monthly Need ÷ Billable Hours  
= $________ ÷ ________ = **$________ /hour**

This is your floor. Never go below it. Compare it honestly with what you charge today.

---

### Method 2: The Market Rate Check

Research what freelancers in your specialty and experience level are charging. Sources:
- Freelancer community forums and subreddits for your specialty
- Job boards that list freelance rates
- Direct conversations with peers (often more accurate than any survey)
- Freelance rate surveys published for your industry

What you're looking for: the range for someone with your experience and specialty. Not the median — the top 25%.

**Market rate range you found:** $________ – $________ /hour  
**Top 25% rate:** $________ /hour

---

### Method 3: The Value-Based Floor

For project work (not hourly), the right question is: what is this project worth to the client?

**Exercise:** For your last 3 projects, estimate:

| Project | What did it enable the client to do? | Rough value to them ($) | What you charged ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

A useful heuristic: if your charge is consistently a tiny fraction (say under 10%) of the value created, you're likely undercharging.

Value-based pricing isn't about charging whatever you want — it's about anchoring your price to impact, not time. If a project will help a client land a $500K contract, charging $2,000 is a bargain, and framing it that way makes the sale easier.

---

### Method 4: The Target Rate

What would you need to earn to feel genuinely good about your business?

Annual income target: $________  
÷ 11 months (1 month buffer for gaps): $________ /month  
÷ Billable hours per month: ________  
= **Target hourly rate: $________ /hour**

---

### Rate Decision

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| Minimum viable rate | $________ |
| Market rate (top 25%) | $________ |
| Value-based check | Confirms / challenges rate? |
| Target rate | $________ |
| **Your rate** | **$________** |

**Rule of thumb:** Set your rate at or above the highest of your minimum viable and target rates. If the market supports it, aim for the top 25%.

**For project-based pricing:** Use your hourly rate to estimate the hours, add 20% buffer, then round to a clean number. Present as a project price, not an hourly rate.

---

### Rate Review Schedule

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Every 12 months | Review and increase by at least inflation |
| After a landmark result | Raise immediately for new clients |
| Consistently closing >75% of proposals | You're probably priced too low |
| Consistently struggling to close | May be a value/fit issue, not just price |
| Raising rates for existing clients | Use prompt D8 |

## Part 2: Winning Proposal Template

Use this structure for any project proposal. Adapt the length to the project — a $500 project needs 1 page; a $20,000 project might need 5.

---

### [YOUR NAME OR BUSINESS NAME]
**Proposal for: [CLIENT NAME]**  
**Date:** [DATE]  
**Valid for:** 14 days

---

### The Situation

*[2–4 sentences. Describe their situation in your words — the context, the problem, and why it matters to them. This shows you listened in discovery and understand their world. This is the most persuasive part of the proposal.]*

Example (fictional):
> "Your sales team is generating more leads than ever, but conversion has stalled at 18% — well below your industry benchmark of 31%. After talking with your team, it's clear the issue isn't the leads: it's that the follow-up email sequence drops off after day 3, leaving most prospects with nothing to respond to."

---

### The Goal

*[1–3 bullet points. What does success look like? Be specific. If you can attach a number, do.]*

- [SPECIFIC OUTCOME 1]
- [SPECIFIC OUTCOME 2]
- [SPECIFIC OUTCOME 3, if applicable]

---

### My Approach

*[Describe your process. This builds confidence — it shows you have a method, not just intentions. 3–6 bullet points or short paragraphs.]*

**Phase 1 – [PHASE NAME] ([TIMEFRAME])**  
[What you do and why]

**Phase 2 – [PHASE NAME] ([TIMEFRAME])**  
[What you do and why]

**Phase 3 – [PHASE NAME] ([TIMEFRAME])**  
[What you do and why]

*Include what you need from the client in each phase. This sets expectations early.*

---

### What's Included

✅ [DELIVERABLE 1]  
✅ [DELIVERABLE 2]  
✅ [DELIVERABLE 3]  
✅ [NUMBER] rounds of revisions  
✅ [ANYTHING ELSE]

**Not included:**  
❌ [EXPLICIT EXCLUSION 1 — e.g., "Copywriting for any pages not listed above"]  
❌ [EXPLICIT EXCLUSION 2 — e.g., "Implementation or development work"]  
❌ [EXPLICIT EXCLUSION 3 — e.g., "Paid advertising strategy or management"]

*Additional work outside this scope can be quoted separately.*

---

### Investment

| Option | What's Included | Investment |
|---|---|---|
| **[OPTION NAME 1]** | [BRIEF DESCRIPTION] | $[PRICE] |
| **[OPTION NAME 2 — Recommended]** | [BRIEF DESCRIPTION] | $[PRICE] |
| **[OPTION NAME 3]** | [BRIEF DESCRIPTION] | $[PRICE] |

*Or, if single option:*

**Total Investment: $[PRICE]**

Payment terms: [E.G., "50% on signing, 50% on delivery" / "100% upfront" / "Monthly billing"]

---

### Timeline

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Project kickoff | [DATE after signing + 3–5 days] |
| [DELIVERABLE 1] | [DATE] |
| [DELIVERABLE 2] | [DATE] |
| Client feedback round | [DATE] |
| Final delivery | [DATE] |

*Timeline begins when signed contract and first payment are received.*

---

### About Me

*[2–4 sentences. Who you are, relevant experience, and one specific result relevant to this type of project. This isn't a biography — it's a confidence-builder.]*

---

### Next Steps

To move forward:

1. Reply to this email with any questions
2. Sign the attached agreement
3. Pay the first invoice ([AMOUNT])

I'll schedule the kickoff call immediately after both are received.

*This proposal is valid for 14 days from the date above. I hold time in my schedule for confirmed projects only.*
