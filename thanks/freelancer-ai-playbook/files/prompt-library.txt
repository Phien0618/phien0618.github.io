==== 1. FIND & PITCH CLIENTS (15 prompts) ====

P1-01 | Define your ideal client in one paragraph
When to use: You pitch everyone and hear back from no one.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Ask me 5 short questions, one at a time, about my 3 best past clients. Then write a one-paragraph ideal-client profile (industry, size, trigger, budget) in under 80 words.
Check 1: Built from your real clients
Check 2: Names a budget range
Check 3: No 'businesses of all sizes'

P1-02 | Research a prospect before you pitch
When to use: You found a company you want to work with and know little about it.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Here is what I found on [Company]: [Research notes]. List 3 likely problems they have that my services solve, each with its evidence, in under 120 words. Label guesses as guesses.
Check 1: Every point has evidence
Check 2: Guesses are labeled
Check 3: Nothing invented about them

P1-03 | Cold email with one specific observation
When to use: You want to email a prospect who has never heard of you.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Write a cold email to [Contact name] at [Company]. Open with this observation: [Specific observation]. Add one relevant proof point and end with one yes/no question. Under 90 words, no flattery.
Check 1: First line is about them
Check 2: One proof point only
Check 3: Ends with a yes/no question

P1-04 | Follow up on a cold email
When to use: Your cold email got no reply after 5-7 days.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Write a follow-up to [Contact name] about my email from [Date sent] on [Topic]. Add one new useful idea instead of a guilt line. Under 60 words, 3 sentences max.
Check 1: No 'just bumping this'
Check 2: Adds one new idea
Check 3: 3 sentences or fewer

P1-05 | Ask a mutual contact for an intro
When to use: Someone you know is connected to a client you want.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Write a note to [Mutual contact] asking for an intro to [Contact name] at [Company], with the reason: [Why them]. Include a 2-sentence blurb they can forward. Under 100 words, easy to say no.
Check 1: Reason is specific
Check 2: Forwardable blurb included
Check 3: Easy to say no

P1-06 | Reply to a posted job (Upwork or job board)

Check 1: Mirrors their goal
Check 2: One relevant result
Check 3: Asks 2 real questions

P1-07 | LinkedIn connection note

Check 1: Under 300 characters
Check 2: No pitch, no link

P1-08 | Turn a past project into a mini case study

Check 2: Client named only if allowed
Check 3: Result is concrete

P1-09 | Write your one-line positioning
When to use: You struggle to answer 'So what do you do?'
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Write 5 one-sentence positioning lines in the form 'I help (who) get (result) with (service)'. Each under 20 words. Then tell me which is clearest and why, in 2 sentences.
Check 1: Names who and the result
Check 2: No buzzwords
Check 3: Under 20 words each

P1-10 | Pitch a current client on a new service
When to use: A current client could use a service they don't buy from you yet.
Prompt: About me: {{profile}} Write an email to [Client name] suggesting [New service] because of [What I noticed]. Give a price range of [Price range] and one next step. Under 110 words, no pressure.

Check 2: Price range is stated
